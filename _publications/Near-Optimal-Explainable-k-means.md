excerpt: 'We study explainable $$k$$-means clustering, introduced by Dasgupta, Frost, Moshkovitz, and Rashtchian (2020), and generalize it to $$\ell_p$$ norms. The goal is to find a clustering represented by a threshold decision tree while minimizing the $$k$$-means objective. Such a clustering is easy for humans to interpret because each internal node partitions the data by thresholding a single feature, so every cluster can be explained through a sequence of simple decisions. We give near-optimal algorithms that use exactly $$k$$ leaves and achieve a price of explainability of $$\tilde{O}(k)$$ for every finite $$p \geq 2$$ and $$\tilde{O}(d^{2/p-1}k)$$ for $$1 \leq p \leq 2$$, as well as bi-criteria algorithms that use $$(1+\delta)k$$ leaves and replace the linear dependence on $$k$$ with polylogarithmic factors. We complement these results with nearly matching lower bounds; in particular, for $$1 \leq p < 2$$ the dependence on the dimension $$d$$ is unavoidable for trees with fewer than $$k^2/2$$ leaves.'
